# YouTube strategy

Written 2026-11-19 (GTA 6 launch day). How we turn other people's streams into something YouTube counts as **ours**, and how the channel is set up.

## 1. The core problem

YouTube pays and pushes channels that add something. A feed of re-cut streamer clips, even credited and captioned, is exactly what the **reused content** rule targets. It's the most common reason clip channels get turned down for monetization, and it gets worse the more the uploads look alike.

So the rule for this channel: **the clip is the raw material, the format is the product.** Every upload should have something no other clip page has: a ranking, a comparison, a count, a theme, a running series, or our own voice.

Quick test before uploading: *If the streamer posted this clip on their own channel, would ours still be worth watching?* If not, add a layer.

## 2. Formats that count as original (ranked by effort vs payoff)

| # | Format | What we add | Shorts | Long-form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Same moment, 4 streamers** | The 4 reactions to one thing (first jet ski, first cop chase, the same NPC) side by side or back to back. | Yes, the best one | Yes, "every big streamer's first hour" |
| 2 | **Top 5 countdown** | Theme + ranking + a reason for each spot on screen ("#3: he paid for it, then crashed it in 4s"). | 45 to 60s, 5 quick moments | 8 to 12 min, top 10 to 15 |
| 3 | **Themed series** with a fixed name and look | Curation + repetition. Viewers come back for the series, not the streamer. | Daily | Weekly roundup |
| 4 | **Found in GTA 6** (tips and discoveries) | Useful info pulled from streams, credited to who found it. This also gets search traffic, which clip channels rarely get. | Yes | "25 things streamers found in week 1" |
| 5 | **Counts and records** | Things we actually counted: "Every time xQc got wasted in hour one (14)". Real numbers only, from the footage. | Yes | Sometimes |
| 6 | **Then vs now** | Same action in GTA V vs GTA 6. Needs GTA V footage we record ourselves. | Yes | Yes |
| 7 | **Your own commentary** | Your real voice reacting to or ranking moments. This is the strongest originality signal YouTube has. | Optional | The best upgrade for long-form |

Number 7 needs a decision: the playbook says "the only voice is the streamer's", which was written for AI voiceovers. **Your own real voice is not AI** and would be the biggest single upgrade. Add it to `decisions.md` if you want it.

### Series ideas (pick 3, keep them for at least a month)

- **Top 5 GTA 6 moments today** (daily Short, the backbone)
- **Top 5 wholesome GTA 6 moments** (streamers being nice to NPCs, saving people, petting animals). Few clip pages do kind moments, so there's less competition.
- **NPC of the day** (the weirdest or funniest NPC interaction)
- **Fail of the day** / **Physics of the day**
- **4 streamers, 1 moment**
- **GTA 6 tip of the day** (credited)
- **Who survived longest** (a five-star wanted level, timed from the footage)

Story moments (wholesome or not) carry the spoiler rules: nothing in the title or thumbnail, "SPOILER" in the description.

## 3. How the uploads fit together

```
Live streams
   │
   ├─ fast lane ─────► single clips (TikTok, Reels, X, a few on Shorts)
   │
   ├─ daily ─────────► 1 to 2 series Shorts (Top 5 today, 4 streamers 1 moment)
   │
   └─ weekly ────────► 1 long video (Top 15 of the week, Found in week 1)
                          └─ every Short links to it ("Related video")
```

**YouTube is not the place for all 20 daily clips.** Posting 20 near-identical clips a day on one YouTube channel is the pattern that gets flagged as reused content. Suggested mix on YouTube:

- **3 to 5 Shorts a day**: 1 to 2 series Shorts + the 2 to 3 strongest single clips.
- **1 to 2 long videos a week** (8 to 12 min). Long-form watch hours are what qualify for monetization faster, and they can't be made from single clips alone.
- TikTok, Reels and X get the full fast-lane volume.

## 4. Shorts that work (on top of the playbook)

- **First frame is the thumbnail.** In the Shorts feed nobody sees a title before deciding. Put the hook and the best facial expression in frame 1.
- **For a ranked Short, put the number in the corner the whole way through** (5, 4, 3...). People stay to see #1.
- **Show #1 last, but tease it at the start** ("#1 broke chat").
- **Loop the ending** into the opening when you can (playbook rule 6).
- **Watch "Viewed vs swiped away"** in YouTube Studio. Under about 60% viewed means the opening is the problem, not the clip.
- **Titles** still count for search and suggestions: `streamer + what happened + GTA 6`, lowercase is fine. Example: `xqc vs the gta 6 cops for 4 minutes straight`.
- Use the **Related video** link on every Short to point at the newest long video.

## 5. Long-form that works

- **Title = search term + promise.** `GTA 6: Top 15 Streamer Moments of Launch Week`, `25 Things Streamers Found in GTA 6's First Day`. People search "GTA 6" + something all week.
- **Thumbnail:** 1 big real face (a streamer's real reaction frame), 1 game frame, 3 words max, numbers help. No AI faces, no Rockstar logos, no story spoilers.
- **Chapters** in the description (`0:00 #15 ...`). They show in search and help retention.
- **First 30 seconds:** show a piece of #1, say what's coming, no intro card.
- **Between moments:** a 1 to 2 second card with the number, the streamer, and why it's ranked there. Those cards are the "added value" a reviewer looks for.
- **Credit every streamer** on screen during their moment and in the description with links.

## 6. Channel setup

### Basics

- [ ] Create the channel as a **Brand Account**, not on your personal Google account, so you can add a helper later and sell or move the channel.
- [ ] **Verify the phone number** (YouTube Studio > Settings > Channel > Feature eligibility). Without it: no custom thumbnails, no videos over 15 min.
- [ ] **Category: Gaming**, and in every upload set **Game title: Grand Theft Auto VI**. That puts the video on the game's page.
- [ ] **Upload defaults** (Settings > Upload defaults): title template, description template with a credit line, tags, category, "Not made for kids". Saves typing on every upload.
- [ ] **Altered or synthetic content: No** (true, since we use real frames only).
- [ ] **Channel keywords:** gta 6, gta vi, gta 6 streamers, gta 6 funny moments, gta 6 tips, leonida, plus your series names.
- [ ] **Playlists:** one per series + "Launch week". Playlists rank in search and lead to binge watching.
- [ ] **Banner + avatar:** your own design, not Rockstar's art or logo. Describe the series and schedule on the banner ("Top 5 daily · Best of the week every Sunday").
- [ ] **About/description:** first line says what the channel is ("Daily GTA 6 streamer moments, ranked and credited"). Add the line "Fan channel. Not affiliated with Rockstar Games." and a takedown contact email.
- [ ] Turn on **comments, the Community tab and the Shorts remix** setting.
- [ ] Never upload a file with a TikTok watermark on it. Upload the clean `02_OUTPUTS` file.

### "US-based account": what helps and what doesn't

The "country of residence" setting has to be where you actually live. YouTube uses it for monetization, AdSense and tax, and a false country can get monetization refused or clawed back later. You don't need it to reach a US audience. What does reach US viewers:

- **Write in American English**: titles, captions, descriptions ("cops", "gas station", "awesome").
- **Post at US times:** Shorts around **12 to 3 pm ET** and **7 to 10 pm ET**. Long videos **Friday to Sunday, about 11 am to 2 pm ET**, so they build up views before the evening.
- **Clip US streamers** (xQc, Kai Cenat, Speed, etc.). Their audience is mostly American.
- Set the **video language and caption language to English (United States)** on every upload.
- Ask your US viewers questions in pinned comments. Early comments from US viewers help the algorithm send it to more US viewers.

If someone in the team really lives in the US, the channel can be registered to them. That's a business decision for you, not a setting.

### Monetization (for planning)

- Full YouTube Partner Program: **1,000 subscribers** plus either **4,000 public watch hours in 12 months** or **10 million Shorts views in 90 days**.
- The main risk on a clip channel is a "reused content" refusal at review. Sections 2 and 3 are the protection: a reviewer opening the channel should see series, rankings and long-form, not a wall of single clips.
- Read Rockstar's video policy before turning monetization on (playbook 4.7).
